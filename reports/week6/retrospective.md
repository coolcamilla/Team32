## What went well

1. **All team members completed their tasks by the established deadlines.** Everyone stayed within their individual timelines.
2. **Mechanics were discussed in detail before implementation began.** Going through the planned mechanics as a team ahead of development helped lock in a shared understanding, so they did not change mid-Sprint.
3. **Tasks were divided and prioritized in a way that avoided blocking dependencies.** Work was sequenced so that no team member had to wait idle for another member's output.

## What did not go well

1. **Programmers did not finish all PBIs in time for the customer meeting, forcing the team to cut 3 PBIs related to UI.** The root cause was uneven time distribution — most of the work was pushed to the final day before the deadline.
2. **Assets already prepared by the artist were not always integrated into the game.** Sprites that were ready in advance were sometimes forgotten during implementation, causing the visual state of the build to lag behind actual development progress.
3. **Motivation dropped across almost the entire team, which affected the quality of the work.** Tasks were still completed, but with less attention to detail and overall polish.

## What changed compared to Sprint 3

1. The team got significantly better at aligning on mechanics before implementation, resolving the misalignment issue flagged in Sprint 3.
2. A clear pull request creation guideline was introduced, reducing merge conflicts. Unlike Sprint 3, where a single error cascaded across six branches, following a defined PR process kept the main branch more stable and cut down on merge-related delays.

## Action points

1. **Introduce mid-Sprint checkpoints for programmers.** For example, by the midpoint of the Sprint each programmer should show partial progress, preventing last-day work and surfacing delays early enough to react.
2. **Add asset integration as an explicit item in the task list.** As soon as the artist finishes an asset, a dedicated task/PBI for integrating it into the game should appear in the task table, so it doesn't fall through the cracks between roles.
3. **Address the drop in motivation directly, e.g. through a short team discussion or 1:1s.** Since it affected nearly everyone, this points to a systemic cause (workload, unclear goals, burnout, deadline pressure) rather than an individual issue - the team lead should identify the source and adjust the process accordingly.
