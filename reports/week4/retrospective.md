## What went well

1. **Regular team communication** The team held more frequent discussions, which helped surface misunderstandings earlier and keep everyone aligned on the current state of work.
2. **The team reached a shared vision for the game.** Disagreements about game content and mechanics were resolved, and the team now has a common understanding of what the game should look and feel like.
3. **Every team member made traceable progress.** All members regularly posted updates about completed workwith evidence, which kept the team informed and made individual contributions visible without requiring separate check-ins.
## What did not go well

1. **Story Points were distributed unevenly across team members.** Some members had significantly more workload than others, which created imbalance and made it harder to deliver everything on time.
2. **Developers lacked a single authoritative source of information for implementation.** Without a unified feature reference, developers sometimes implemented features differently from what the team expected, leading to rework and misalignment.
3. **The team barely met the deadline due to inaccurate task estimation.** Several PBIs took longer than estimated, which compressed the time available for review and verification at the end of the Sprint.

## What changed compared to Sprint 1

Based on the Sprint 1 retrospective, the team introduced a daily messages in the team chat so the team lead could track progress without separate check-ins, and began PBI refinement earlier

## Action points

1. **Establish a single feature list as the authoritative reference for developers.** Before implementation begins, the team will agree on one document that describes the expected behaviour of each feature in sufficient detail, so developers have a clear and shared source of truth.
2. **Conduct Planning Poker collaboratively for all Sprint PBIs.** Estimation will be done as a group discussion rather than individually, so the team can surface complexity and risk earlier and produce more accurate Story Point estimates.
